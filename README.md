# Product Playground

Build a premium personal web application called:

PRODUCT LAB

Notes, experiments & product thinking by Ruchi Parikh

This is an interactive product-thinking portfolio.

It should NOT look like a traditional resume website.

It should NOT claim that I am currently a Product Manager.

It should NOT present hypothetical work as professional PM experience.

The purpose is to demonstrate how I think about products through:

product analysis

independent product exercises

product teardowns

metrics thinking

prioritization

AI/product exploration

experiments

reflections on things I learn from strong product practitioners and products

The site should feel like a thoughtful product journal combined with a small interactive product lab.

HOME — FEATURED NOTE

Feature one product note prominently.

Initial featured note:

Don't Copy the Feature. Understand the Mechanism.

Subtitle:

What the Duolingo growth story changed about how I think about product ideas

Label:

Inspired by:
Jorge Mazal's “How Duolingo reignited user growth” in Lenny's Newsletter.

IMPORTANT:

Do NOT reproduce the article.

Do NOT copy paragraphs.

Do NOT make this a summary of the article.

Instead create an original personal reflection.

The central idea to explore:

A feature that works well in one product may fail somewhere else because the underlying user behavior, motivation, context, incentives, and product mechanics are different.

The Duolingo story is useful as an example because the team initially borrowed ideas from other products, experienced failures, reflected on why they failed, and later became more systematic about understanding the mechanism and context behind successful product ideas.

Write the reflection in my own voice.

Include:

What caught my attention

What I took away

Where I think people can get this wrong

A fictional example

What I would ask before copying a feature

What I would measure

Make it thoughtful and conversational.

Cite/link the original article as the source.

PRIORITIZATION LAB

Create a fictional scenario:

“You have six possible improvements and limited engineering capacity. What would you prioritize?”

Allow the visitor to adjust:

Business value
Effort
Confidence

Show how the prioritization changes.

Do NOT label a result as:
“Correct answer”

Instead:

“Your prioritization”

Then:

“What changed?”

Explain the trade-offs.

Clearly label:

Fictional Product Scenario

METRICS LAB

Create a fictional scenario:

“Weekly active users dropped unexpectedly.”

Allow visitors to explore:

Acquisition
Activation
Engagement
Retention

Each category reveals questions they might investigate.

For example:

Activation:

Did onboarding completion change?

Did the change affect a particular segment?

Did something recently change?

Retention:

Are existing users returning less often?

Is the problem concentrated in a particular cohort?

Do not provide fake real-world numbers.

This is a thinking exercise.

MVP LAB

Create an interactive fictional product idea.

Present 8 possible features.

Let the visitor choose which features belong in the MVP.

Then display:

MVP
Deferred
Why

Explain the trade-offs.

Important message:

The goal of an MVP is not simply to build less. It is to learn something important with the smallest useful product.

Keep the explanation natural and not textbook-like.

CONTENT QUALITY CONTROL

Before displaying any content, ask:

Is this actually true?

Is this my experience or a hypothetical exercise?

If hypothetical, is that clearly labeled?

Does this demonstrate product thinking?

Does this sound like a human wrote it?

Is this specific enough to be interesting?

Am I claiming something I cannot prove?

If something fails, simplify it.

SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION

When an article, podcast, newsletter, or external product is used as inspiration:

Clearly show:

Inspired by / Source

Provide a link to the original source.

Do not reproduce large portions of copyrighted content.

Summarize concepts in original language and add my own interpretation.

The goal is not to create a “Lenny Newsletter summary website.”

The goal is:

I learned something → I thought about it → I formed my own view → I applied it to a new scenario.

FINAL IMPRESSION

The visitor should not leave thinking:

“Here is someone who made an AI-generated PM portfolio.”

“This person is curious about products, can articulate their thinking, understands trade-offs, learns from strong product examples, and has deliberately built the foundations for a move into Product Management.”

The application itself should demonstrate those qualities.
