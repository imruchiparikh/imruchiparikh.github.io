# Product Lab implementation plan

## Direction
Build a multi-page editorial product journal with a restrained ink, paper, moss, and signal-red palette. Use strong typography, generous whitespace, fine rules, numbered entries, and subtle motion. Keep the visual identity original and avoid resume-template patterns, stock imagery, gradients, glass effects, and oversized illustration.

## Shared experience
- Create a compact header reading “Ruchi Parikh | Product Lab” with an accessible Explore dropdown grouped exactly as requested.
- Add separate routes for Home, Product Notes, Product Teardowns, What I Changed My Mind About, Prioritization Lab, Metrics Lab, MVP Lab, My Path to Product, and Certifications.
- Include external Resume and LinkedIn actions as clearly labeled placeholders until real URLs are supplied; do not reproduce the resume.
- Add route-specific titles and sharing descriptions throughout.
- Use reusable editorial building blocks for page introductions, source attribution, draft labels, fictional-scenario labels, note rows, and footers.

## Home
- Keep the opening concise with the exact Product Lab positioning and an Explore the Lab action.
- Feature “Don’t Copy the Feature. Understand the Mechanism.” prominently.
- Add a compact index of notes, experiments, learning sources, and the “This site is also an experiment” statement without turning the page into a career summary.

## Thinking pages
- Product Notes: create the six requested entries as an editorial index, then expand with additional source-inspired pieces (more Lenny's Newsletter and Lenny's Podcast reflections) and book reflections for The Mom Test, Think Straight, Build What Matters, and Smart Brevity. Each is an original takeaway in Ruchi's voice — never a summary. Write complete original content where it relies on public product reasoning; mark experience-dependent pieces as editable drafts.
- Featured Duolingo reflection: cover what caught attention, takeaway, likely misuse, a fictional example, validation questions, and measures. Attribute and link the original Lenny’s Newsletter article without reproducing it.
- Product Teardowns: start with one replaceable, clearly observational teardown. Separate observed behavior from hypotheses and unknowns.
- Changed My Mind: add 2–3 modest editable entries using the requested before/now/reflection structure.
- Add an “Inspired by” synthesis pattern and a “What I’m Learning From” shelf focused on takeaway rather than summaries.

## Interactive labs
- Prioritization Lab: present six fictional improvements; let visitors adjust user value, business value, effort, and confidence; recalculate and explain their resulting order and trade-offs.
- Metrics Lab: present the fictional weekly-active-user drop; let visitors explore Acquisition, Activation, Engagement, and Retention questions without fabricated data.
- MVP Lab: present eight fictional features, allow selection into MVP versus Deferred, and generate a natural explanation of the learning trade-off.
- Clearly label every lab as a fictional product scenario and never imply a correct answer or professional case study.

## About pages
- My Path to Product: use only the supplied career facts and exact progression, ending with “Continuing to build product thinking.”
- Certifications: keep CDPM and CSPO understated and factual.

## Writing and truthfulness checks
- Avoid prohibited job claims and corporate/AI phrasing.
- Use first-person observations, uncertainty, questions, and concrete examples.
- Label editable drafts, hypothetical exercises, observations, assumptions, and unknowns explicitly.
- Do not invent employers, outcomes, user research, feedback, launches, metrics, or personal anecdotes.

## Validation
- Check all routes, dropdown links, keyboard navigation, sliders/selectors, and mobile layouts.
- Verify there is one clear heading per page, no clipped or overlapping text, and no runtime or build errors.
- Review every claim against the supplied facts before completion.
